Vocet Dictation

Privacy policy

For Vocet Dictation 0.2.0 and later on Mac. Last updated 20 November 2026.

In short

  • Vocet has no server that receives your dictations, and no account with us exists. We never see your audio or your words.
  • On this Mac: audio and text stay on your Mac. Only the one-time model download comes from Hugging Face.
  • Connect OpenRouter: your audio and text go from your Mac to OpenRouter and the model providers behind it, under your own OpenRouter account. We ask them not to collect your data.
  • We don't use your recordings or text to train any model, and we ask providers not to.
  • Usage and error reports are on by default, carry a random install ID, and never hold your audio, words or keys. You can turn them off in Settings → Privacy.

Who we are

Vocet Dictation is made by Ankit Garg, Vocet Labs. Questions, or want to know what we hold about you? Write to vocetlabs@gmail.com.

What leaves your Mac, by mode

On this Mac Connect OpenRouter
Your audio Stays on your Mac. Sent to OpenRouter, which passes it to OpenAI (gpt-4o-mini-transcribe) to turn it into text.
The text, and your custom words (if you have any) Stays on your Mac. Sent to OpenRouter, which passes it to a cleanup model (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash) that removes filler words, false starts and stammers and fixes clearly misheard technical words. If no host answers within 1.5 seconds, nothing is cleaned and your text is pasted as it came.
Settings we send with every request None, nothing is sent. data_collection: deny on every request (use only providers that don't collect data). zdr: true on cleanup requests (ask for zero-data-retention hosts; see below for what your OpenRouter account setting decides).
Who handles it Nobody but you. OpenRouter and the provider behind each step, under your OpenRouter account and its terms. Vocet is not in the path.

Your OpenRouter key stays in your macOS Keychain and is sent only to OpenRouter. Signing in with Connect OpenRouter sends nothing about you to us.

What providers keep

We state only what the providers publish today, and link it. Where a period isn't published for our exact route, we don't guess one: what they keep is governed by OpenRouter's and the provider's own policies.

  • OpenRouter says it doesn't store your prompts or responses unless you turn on prompt logging or the separate "use my inputs/outputs" opt-in (both off by default), or content is flagged as possible child sexual abuse material (screened on all requests, including zero-retention ones; flagged content may be kept for review and legal reasons). It does keep metadata such as token counts and timing. Some endpoints marked "Safety Retention" may keep prompts and responses to prevent abuse (not for training). Whether a request can reach them follows your OpenRouter account's settings: our zdr: true request follows the account's choice, and OpenRouter's documentation says self-serve accounts allow Safety Retention by default. To exclude them, turn on "ZDR only" in your OpenRouter privacy settings. So we can't guarantee zero retention for your requests. Sources: data collection, zero data retention.
  • OpenAI (speech to text) says API data isn't used for training by default, and its table lists no abuse-monitoring retention for the audio transcription endpoint when called directly. How long the copy that comes through OpenRouter is kept is governed by OpenRouter's and OpenAI's policies; we can't promise a number. Source: OpenAI, your data.
  • The cleanup host is chosen by OpenRouter. With "ZDR only" on in your account, it uses hosts OpenRouter lists as zero data retention (no routine storage after processing, apart from the screening above); otherwise a Safety Retention host may be used. Source: OpenRouter ZDR.
  • Hugging Face serves the one-time model downloads. It sees an ordinary download request (your IP address), not your audio. See its privacy policy.

No training

Vocet doesn't use your recordings or text to train models. It asks providers not to collect or train on them (data_collection: deny). If we ever want to use recordings, it will be a separate opt-in that we announce first; nothing like that exists in this version.

Usage and error reports

On by default, with one off switch in Settings → Privacy ("Share usage and error events"). Off takes effect at once, deletes what was waiting and your random install ID, and can't recall what was already sent.

  • PostHog Cloud (US) gets a closed list of events, each with a random install ID made on your Mac (it links one installation's events; it isn't your name, account or hardware, and switching reports off deletes it). The events: the app opened (version, macOS version, chip), setup steps reached, which engine you chose, that a dictation worked or failed (length and speed in rough ranges, the kind of failure), which setting changed (not its value), and that the app updated. No profiles, no IP address.
  • Sentry (US) gets a report when the app crashes or a dictation fails. A crash report includes the app version, the macOS major version, an error code and a stack trace: function names and source-file locations from Vocet, the libraries it uses and the Rust standard library, as they were when the app was built. It never includes the error message, paths to your files, your audio, dictated text, keys or app names.
  • Setup is the exception to the switch. The switch appears on the last setup screen, so the setup events (steps reached, where setup stopped, and fixed reasons such as a failed download or a refused permission) are sent before you see it, unless a choice you made earlier is stored. Nothing about what you say is among them.

The full list of every event and field is in docs/privacy.md, and the code enforces it.

What stays on your Mac

  • History: your last 25 dictations by default (Off, 10, 25, 50 or 100), text only, in a file only your user can read. Never sent anywhere. Delete one, clear all, or turn it off in Settings → History.
  • Audio is kept in memory and dropped after use. Release builds never save it. Only a developer debug build, started with a special flag, saves recordings as WAV files in ~/Library/Application Support/com.dictationapp.desktop/debug-recordings. They never leave the Mac and are separate from History: clearing History doesn't remove them. To delete them, quit Vocet and move that folder to the Trash.
  • Your key lives in the macOS Keychain.
  • Logs never hold transcripts or keys.

Update checks

The app asks GitHub for a small file that names the latest version (github.com/…/releases/latest/download/latest.json). Vocet adds nothing personal to that request; GitHub sees it as it sees any download.

Your choices

  • Turn reports off: Settings → Privacy.
  • Delete history: Settings → History.
  • Stop cloud use: switch to On this Mac, or Disconnect OpenRouter in Settings → Speech. You can also delete the key at openrouter.ai.
  • Uninstall the app by dragging it to the Trash.
  • Ask about data: email vocetlabs@gmail.com. We hold no account for you, and usage events carry only a random ID, so we may not be able to find yours.

Children

Vocet isn't directed at children under 13 (under 16 in the EU).

Changes

We'll date every change on this page and announce material ones in the release notes before they apply.
