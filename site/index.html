Two ways to run

No Vocet account and no Vocet server: your audio goes through your own OpenRouter account, or never leaves your Mac. Read the privacy policy.

Where your data goes

Connect OpenRouter

OpenRouter sends your audio to OpenAI to turn it into text and sends the text to a cleanup model on a zero-data-retention host. Vocet doesn’t run a server that receives your dictations.

On this Mac

Your audio and dictation text stay on your Mac.

Questions

What does it cost?

Vocet is free during the beta. On this Mac there is nothing else to pay. With Connect OpenRouter you pay OpenRouter for what you use: about $0.70 per 1,000 dictations, with a $5 minimum top-up.

What does Vocet clean up?

Vocet removes filler words (um, uh, you know), false starts and stammers, writes spoken email addresses and links as symbols (sam at example dot com becomes sam@example.com), and fixes clearly misheard technical words (get hub becomes GitHub). It keeps your other words and self-corrections exactly as you said them.

Why does Vocet need Accessibility access?

To notice the dictation key, paste for you, and check the one character before your cursor for spacing. It never reads whole fields, password fields or your screen.

What if my text doesn’t paste?

Choose Copy last dictation from the Vocet menu (or open History for the last 25) to paste it yourself. Vocet skips password fields.

Can I see the source code?

Yes, on GitHub. It is source available under the Functional Source License. You can read and build the code. Each release is available under FSL-1.1-ALv2 and becomes available under Apache 2.0 two years after it is published.
