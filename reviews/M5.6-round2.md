## Known limit and user impact

The union of the two reads deliberately cannot distinguish a device bit hidden
by the synthetic chord from a real Command released during the paste. In the
latter case cleanup can restore a stale Command bit. This is acceptable as a
documented residual risk for this fix: it requires a physical release between
the snapshot and cleanup, a short sequence of synchronous calls with no explicit
wait. That window is normally narrow, although scheduling can extend it; no
frequency or maximum duration was measured.

The consequence can be disruptive: until corrected, synthetic input inheriting
session flags can invoke shortcuts instead of typing, as in #140. The next real
Command press/release supplies authoritative side bits and corrects the state.
An ordinary next keypress is **not a verified recovery guarantee**: source-less
synthetic events can inherit the contaminated state, and the hotkey listener
consults session flags when ordinary event flags disagree. I do not assume all
ordinary physical keypresses repair every consumer. This residual timing case
does not invalidate the now-covered sustained-hold use case; it remains an
explicit limitation rather than a claim that modifier transitions are race-free.
